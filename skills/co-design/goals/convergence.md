# Convergence

Each choice is made against stated criteria, and says what it gives up, how sure it is and what would change it.

*Falls short:* never closing. *Overshoots:* closing before the options were compared.

## Desirable

- **Decisiveness:** it ends in a recommendation or a short ranked list, not a survey.
- **Explicit criteria:** the standards for judging are stated and tied to the stated goal.
- **Fair comparison:** every option gets the same criteria and the same scrutiny.
- **Evidence-based:** judgments rest on facts, constraints and stated priorities, not preference or momentum.
- **Trade-off clarity:** what each choice gives up is named.
- **Synthesis:** strong parts of rejected options go into the winner where that helps.
- **Traceability:** each eliminated option has a brief reason.
- **Calibrated confidence:** the recommendation says how sure it is and what would change it.
- **Actionability:** the outcome includes a next step, not only a verdict.
- **Reversibility awareness:** choices hard to undo get more care than cheap ones.

## Undesirable

- **Premature closure:** settling before the alternatives are compared.
- **Anchoring:** favoring the first, most recent or most familiar option.
- **Fence-sitting:** "it depends" with no recommendation.
- **Hidden criteria:** a choice that doesn't show what it was judged on.
- **Uneven scrutiny:** the favorite's flaws are glossed over and the rivals' picked apart.
- **Dropped objections:** raised objections are neither resolved nor recorded.
- **Mushy compromise:** a blend that keeps no option's strengths.
- **Analysis paralysis:** weighing continues after the criteria separate the options.
- **Overconfidence:** a close call presented as an obvious winner.
- **Discarding value:** useful ideas are thrown away with the options they came from.
- **Deference:** choosing what the user named or praised when the criteria don't support it.
