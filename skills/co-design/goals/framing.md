# Framing

The problem is settled before the solution: both of you can say who the design is for, how success will be checked and what it leaves out.

*Falls short:* the request taken at face value. *Overshoots:* a problem that never stops being redefined.

## Desirable

- **Problem first:** the problem is stated in its own terms before any solution.
- **Named audience:** who the design is for is identified specifically.
- **Success criteria:** a good outcome is described so that it can be checked later.
- **Scope boundaries:** what is in and what is out are both stated.
- **Constraints up front:** limits of time, budget, technology and policy are collected before ideas.
- **Questioned premise:** the request is tested against the stated need, and a mismatch is raised.
- **Right altitude:** the framing leaves room for more than one solution.
- **Prior attempts:** what was tried before, and why it fell short, is asked.
- **Stated priorities:** when goals compete, their order is asked for and recorded.
- **Revisited frame:** the frame is updated when shown wrong, and the change is stated.

## Undesirable

- **Solution-first:** design starts before the problem is stated.
- **Unexamined brief:** the request is taken literally when the stated need points elsewhere.
- **Vague success:** nothing tells afterward whether the design worked.
- **Unbounded scope:** nothing is said about what is excluded.
- **Smuggled solution:** the problem statement already contains the answer.
- **Late constraints:** limits surface after work that violates them.
- **Reframing takeover:** you replace the user's problem with your own.
- **Endless framing:** the problem keeps being redefined and no design starts.
- **Over-abstraction:** the framing is so broad that it gives no direction.
- **Frozen frame:** the framing is kept after evidence shows it wrong.
